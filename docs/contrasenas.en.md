# Passwords

A password is **the key to your digital home**. If it is weak or you use it everywhere, it's like having the same key for your car, flat and workplace.

## What is a bad password

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
|---|---|
| `123456`, `password`, `qwerty` | A long, personal phrase |
| Your name or date of birth | Something nobody can guess |
| The same one on every account | A different one for each account |
| The shorter, the worse | The longer, the better |

## Step 1: create a "passphrase"

The easiest way is to use **four or more unrelated words**, with a number or symbol. It is easy to remember and very hard to guess.

!!! example "Example (don't use this one)"
    `Cloud-Turtle-Table-47!`

Quick rules:

1. At least **12 characters** (16 is better).
2. Mix letters, numbers and a symbol.
3. **Don't use personal data** (name, pet, date).
4. **A different password** for each important account (email, bank, social media).

!!! danger "The most important one: your email"
    Whoever gets into your email can request a password reset for **all** your other accounts. Give it your strongest password.

## Step 2: use a password manager

Remembering 30 long passwords is impossible. A **password manager** stores them for you: you only remember **one** master password.

=== "Windows"

    Options: your browser's built-in manager (Edge or Chrome) with a protected profile, or a dedicated manager such as **Bitwarden**.

=== "macOS"

    Options: **iCloud Keychain / Passwords** (already on your Mac), or a dedicated manager such as **Bitwarden**.

## Step 3: turn on two-step verification

It is the **most effective** measure you can take. Besides the password, it asks for a code sent to your phone.

1. Go to the security settings of your email account.
2. Look for **"Two-step verification"** (or "Two-factor authentication").
3. Click **Turn on** and follow the instructions.
4. Keep the **recovery codes** on paper, in a safe place.

- [ ] Two-step verification on my email
- [ ] Two-step verification on my bank
- [ ] Two-step verification on my social media

??? question "What if I lose my phone?"
    That's why the **recovery codes** are kept on paper. With them you can get back in and set up a new phone.

??? question "Is it safe to write passwords in a notebook?"
    At home, locked away, it is better than using the same password everywhere. But a password manager is the recommended option.

## Typical mistakes

- Using the same password "with a different number at the end".
- Sharing passwords over WhatsApp.
- Saving keys in unprotected phone notes.
- Leaving your session open on public computers.
